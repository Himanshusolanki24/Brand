# The Proofing Desk — BrandProof's design philosophy

BrandProof is not a compliance dashboard. It is a publisher's proofroom, rebuilt for
the age of AI drafts: a quiet desk where a brand's words are laid out, marked up in
colored pencil, argued over once, and signed off by a human.

## Ink and paper, not glass and neon

The app sits on near-white proofing paper (`#FBFAF7`) lit like a clean worktable.
Text is written in blue-black ink (`#1C2536`) — not the flat #0B0B0B of a default
theme. Color is reserved for three instruments a proofroom actually owns:

- **Red pen** `#C2402A` — the annotator's marking color. Every flagged word,
  high issue, and "claim needs proof" is marked in red pen, the way a
  proofreader has always worked.
- **Green ink stamp** `#1F6B4A` — approval is a physical act. Signed, stamped,
  dated. Never implied, never automatic.
- **Citation blue** `#2457C5` — every issue that claims authority must carry a
  link to the exact clause that grants it. Blue is the hyperlink's honest color;
  an issue without a blue link admits it is advisory.

Pencil amber `#9A6A1F` marks medium severity; graphite `#6B7280` marks what is
merely worth a look.

## The human hand is allowed in, at the edges

The interface is crisp, but it leans on the proofroom's vernacular: a slightly
wobbled underline in the hero, hand-sketched diagrams (the same napkin vocabulary
as the Round 1 plan), the Clarity Radar drawn as if with a 0.7 mm fineliner, and
Caveat handwriting used only where a human would genuinely write — annotations,
empty states, the signature on an approval. Written notes are content here; a
reviewer's scribble is the product's soul. It never decorates a control.

## Documents, not cards

Content is reviewed as a **document laid on the desk** — a page with margins,
section headings, and line breaks — not as a feed of identical rounded cards.
Issues float beside it on the proofing tray, each with a different height, like
strips of masking tape holding notes. Severity, confidence, the cited clause,
the suggested fix, and the three human verdicts (Accept / Edit / Dismiss with a
reason) are always visible without opening anything.

## Restraint ledger

- One memorable element per screen (the document, the radar, the stamp).
- No gradients, no glassmorphism, no shadow soup, no rounded-everything.
- Motion only when it answers a hand: a stamp lands, a verdict counts down.
- Type: Bricolage Grotesque for display (a voice with an opinion), Instrument
  Sans for the working UI, Caveat for the human margin notes.
